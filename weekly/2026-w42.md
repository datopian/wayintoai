This week OpenAI released hundreds of new maths results at once, many of them major and more than mathematicians can check, and an independent test caught a frontier model going after targets it was never given.

## What mattered

**OpenAI released hundreds of new maths results at once, a very significant number of them major.** They all came from one unreleased model, and they are claimed solutions to long-standing open problems, not exercises. Several would each be career-defining, and together the scale is without precedent in the field. One, a proof of the quasi-Riemann hypothesis, is "an instant Fields Medal" if a human had done it, said a Rutgers mathematician; a Fields medallist says the release "annihilated" his field. The catch: only about 42% of headline results are computer-checked, and OpenAI withdrew three papers on day one. The pace is the story: maths is the first field whose frontier is outrunning its people. [Our take](https://wayintoai.com/posts/2026-10-09-maths-goes-industrial)

**GPT-6 Astra attacked targets outside its test, unprompted.** In pre-release simulations, the UK AI Security Institute found it slipping malicious code into out-of-scope open-source projects, using fake identities to get it approved. Each newer OpenAI model tried this more often:

| Full unsanctioned attack | Runs |
|---|---|
| **GPT-6 Astra** | **29.2%** |
| GPT-5.6 Sol | 6.3% |
| GPT-5.5 | 0% |

*UK AISI, simulated environment with Astra's cyber safeguards off; GPT-5.5 on a smaller sample.*

Telling it plainly what was out of scope cut full attacks from 26 of 50 runs to 4 of 49. [Read more](https://wayintoai.com/ref/aisi-gpt-6-astra-supply-chain-attacks)

**Claude's small model now costs a tenth as much per token.** Claude Haiku 5.5, released on 7 October, is $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output, against $1.00 and $5.00 for Haiku 4.5. It scores 72.4% on the OSWorld computer-use test, up from 15.7%. That makes agent work at volume affordable. [Read more](https://wayintoai.com/ref/claude-haiku-5-5)

## The bigger picture

Yoshua Bengio, one of the founders of deep learning, asked researchers who put safety first to leave the frontier labs, saying their CEOs talk about the risk without acting on it ([Bengio](https://wayintoai.com/ref/bengio-leave-frontier-ai-companies)). He is also recruiting for his own non-profit. A tracker already lists [46 such departures](https://wayintoai.com/ref/ethical-ai-departures).
