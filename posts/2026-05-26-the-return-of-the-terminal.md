Forty years ago, all you had was the terminal. It was *the* interface to computing -- simple, text-based, easy for a computer to parse, and extremely efficient. Then we built GUIs because humans are visual beings.

![black flat screen computer monitor](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1608742213509-815b97c30b36?fm=jpg&q=60&w=3000&auto=format&fit=crop&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&ixid=M3wxMjA3fDB8MHxwaG90by1wYWdlfHx8fGVufDB8fHx8fA%3D%3D)

As Neal Stephenson wrote, “In the Beginning was the Command Line.” Now, for the first time in a long while, we have a genuinely new interface: the conversation. We talk. Maybe we draw occasionally, but mainly we just talk. And talking turns out to be extremely efficient.

This means the terminal is back. I don’t need a GUI to do most things anymore. I can just dictate, and that becomes my interface to nearly everything. The conversation \*is\* the terminal.

There’s a deeper reason for this return. Command-line tools are very efficient for AI, and they’re faster. An AI doesn’t care about a GUI -- in fact, a GUI is \*harder\* for an AI to process. The same properties that made the terminal powerful in 1985 -- text in, text out, composable, scriptable -- make it the natural interface for AI in 2026.

We’ve come full circle.
